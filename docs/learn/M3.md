## Concepts

- **The EVM is a stack machine.** Opcodes pop and push words. A raw `ADD` wraps around where Solidity 0.8 reverts, and an invalid opcode or an empty stack burns all the gas while `REVERT` keeps what is left.
- **A call is found by its first four bytes.** The selector is the start of the hash of the signature, and the rest of the calldata is one word per argument. A selector that matches nothing runs the fallback or reverts.
- **A revert undoes the call that reverted, and the access list with it.** A caller that catches it keeps its own earlier writes, and a slot touched inside the reverted call is cold again.
- **Call, delegatecall and staticcall differ in whose storage and sender they use.** Delegatecall runs the code against the caller's storage by slot, not by name, which is what the later account modules rely on.
- **State has a price list.** A first write to an empty slot costs 22100 with the cold access, a change to a filled one 5000, and a read 2100 or 100. A refund is capped at a fifth of the gas used, memory grows quadratically, a log costs 375 plus 375 a topic plus 8 a byte, and transient storage costs 100.
- **Calldata is priced twice.** 4 gas a zero byte and 16 a non-zero one, unless the call does so little that the EIP-7623 floor of 10 a token is higher.
- **ERC-20 is a ledger inside a contract.** The standard leaves open the zero address, an infinite allowance and the order of checks, and each choice is a trap or a saving.
- **Properties are checked three ways.** A unit test checks one scenario, a fuzz test checks one function with random input, and an invariant test checks a property after any sequence of calls, with a handler and a model so the sequences mean something.

## What the tests taught

- **S1:** a call from the test contract costs about 21,200 gas even when the code is a single `STOP`. This stayed unexplained until S6.
- **S2:** the optimizer deletes a read whose result is unused and merges two writes to one slot, so a gas meter built on them measured 2 gas instead of 2100. The values have to be used. A refund came back as 8,880 instead of 19,900 because EIP-3529's cap of a fifth of the gas used applied, and `forge coverage` builds without the optimizer, so every bound has to hold in both builds.
- **S3:** the approve race is real: a spender who acts between an allowance of 50 and one of 30 spends 80.
- **S4:** OpenZeppelin refuses the zero address, never spends a maximum allowance and reports ERC-6093 errors. It also keeps the approve race.
- **S5:** the invariant that the zero address holds nothing was vacuous until four actions targeted it. Foundry replays a persisted failing sequence first, which made a check I had written fail on every short replay and made the first round of mutation results meaningless. The check became a deterministic test and the cache is cleared between mutants.
- **S6:** Foundry charges a call that the test contract makes like a transaction: the 21,000 base and the calldata, with no cold-access charge for the contract it calls. That explains S1's figure, and calls between contracts are not charged. A near-empty call with 100 non-zero bytes costs 3,000 more than with 100 zero bytes, which is the EIP-7623 floor at 30 a byte, while a call that does real work pays the 12 a byte of the standard price. A transfer to a fresh address costs exactly 17,100 more than to a holder, and OpenZeppelin costs within about 33 gas of the bare token on a transfer. Its skipped allowance write saves about 3,200 gas on a spend with a maximum allowance.

## Check questions

1. **How a call runs.** Why does `ADD` on the maximum wrap around in raw bytecode but revert in Solidity 0.8? What do `INVALID` and `REVERT` each do to the gas left? If a caller catches a reverting inner call, which of its writes survive, and what does the revert do to the gas price of a slot the inner call touched?
2. **What state costs.** Why does a first write to an empty slot cost 22,100 while a change to a filled one costs 5,000 and a read costs 100? Why does a transfer to a fresh address cost 17,100 more than one to a holder? What is the refund for clearing a slot, and why was a refund of 19,900 reported as 8,880?
3. **ERC-20 traps.** What does EIP-20 leave open that the bare token got wrong in a way that matters, and how does OpenZeppelin fix it? How does the approve race work, and what should a client do about it? What is the trade-off of an infinite allowance, in gas and in risk?
4. **Testing a property.** What is the difference between a unit, a fuzz and an invariant test? Why does an invariant test need a handler and a reference model, and why is `fail_on_revert` on? What went wrong with the first round of mutation results, and what does that say about trusting a passing test?
5. **Gas from a test.** Why does a call from the test contract cost about 21,000 gas more than the same call from another contract? When does the calldata floor of EIP-7623 decide the price and when does the standard price? Why must a gas bound hold in the unoptimized build too?
